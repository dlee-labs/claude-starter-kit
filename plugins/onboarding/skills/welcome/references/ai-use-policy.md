# [ORG NAME] — Policy on the use of AI

**Starter draft for leadership.** Adapted from a church's adopted policy and generalized for a church and
school. Replace every `[BRACKETED]` value. Section 9 lists the decisions that belong to leadership, not IT.
The AI-facing version of this policy is `ai-rules.md`; when the two disagree, this document governs.

## 1. Why we are writing this

AI tools can give our staff back hours every week for the parts of the work only people can do: teaching,
caring for students and families, sitting with someone in a hard week. They can also expose people,
especially children, if used carelessly. This policy says what we use, for what, and where the lines are.

AI handles the work *around* our mission, never the mission itself. A tool that saves six hours and quietly
replaces a conversation with a parent has cost more than it saved.

## 2. Who this binds, and the terms we use

All staff and volunteers using AI for [ORG NAME] work, on any device. "Sanctioned tools" are the AI products
and connectors listed in `ai-rules.md` rule 29. "Consumer AI" is any personal or free AI account.

Students are not users of the staff AI tools. Student-facing AI, if ever adopted, needs its own policy.

## 3. Our convictions

1. People are not data points. We work with information to serve people, never to rank or expose them.
2. A human owns every outcome. AI drafts and proposes; a named person decides and answers for it.
3. Children get the most protection, and decisions about a child are always made by a responsible adult.
4. We prefer doing less to doing harm. When unsure, stop and ask.
5. Refusing permitted work is also a failure. Most of our work is nowhere near these lines.

## 4. Our data, in four tiers

Sort once, and the right rule follows.

| Tier | Examples | What AI may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Website, calendars, handbooks, published newsletters, sermon audio | Freely, in any tool |
| **Internal** | Policies in draft, schedules, network diagrams, aggregate counts, device inventory without names | In sanctioned tools |
| **Confidential** | Staff and student directory, rosters, account lists, access and audit logs, monitoring exports, device assignments | In sanctioned tools, by staff whose role already grants that access, for that work. Never in consumer AI |
| **Restricted** | Counseling, discipline, special-education and health records; HR files; individual giving | Never in general-purpose use. Each has its own rule in `ai-rules.md` |

**AI access tracks existing access and never exceeds it.** If your role lets you see it in the system of
record, you may work with it in the sanctioned tools. If it does not, AI does not change that.

**Files that mix tiers** take the tier of the most sensitive thing in them. AI reads whole files, including
hidden tabs and comments. When a task needs part of such a file, copy only what it needs, as values, into a
new blank file, and use that.

## 5. Student activity and safety monitoring

Our filtering and monitoring tools ([GOGUARDIAN / GOOGLE ADMIN AUDIT LOGS / OTHER]) produce records of what
students do on school devices and accounts. AI can help a trained staff member find patterns in those
records faster. Rules:

- **Purpose:** safety and acceptable-use review only, as defined in [ACCEPTABLE USE POLICY]. Not general
  profiling, not academic evaluation.
- **Who:** only [ROLES], working in the sanctioned workspace.
- **How:** exports are analyzed locally in a project folder, findings cite the rows they come from, and
  nothing is concluded about a child by the tool. A named adult reviews every finding before any action.
- **No rankings or risk scores** of students.
- **Safety first:** anything suggesting harm to a child goes immediately to [SAFETY PROCEDURE / MANDATED
  REPORTER], not into further analysis.
- **Retention:** raw exports and working files are deleted [N] days after the review closes; the finding
  and its outcome are kept in [SYSTEM OF RECORD].
- **Legal review:** [LEADERSHIP / COUNSEL] confirms how student-privacy law (FERPA, COPPA, CIPA and any
  state law) applies to this use before it starts.

## 6. Safeguards, in plain language

1. **The paid, protected tool.** Our AI runs on a business plan contractually barred from training on our
   data. Consumer AI makes no such promise, which is why it is banned for org data.
2. **AI proposes; a person approves; then it happens.** Every send, post, or change is shown in full and
   approved in the conversation first. Email is draft-only.
3. **Narrow keys.** Tools that touch our systems hold keys scoped to their job, read-only where possible.
   Personal sign-ins stay with the person in the password manager. Org-wide keys stay on [ALWAYS-ON SERVER]
   behind a service that logs every call.
4. **Everything leaves a receipt.** Tool access is logged; bulk changes keep their per-item list.
5. **Work worth keeping is saved where the team can find it.** Chat is a workbench, not an archive.
6. **Aggregates for outsiders.** Anything for people without the same access uses counts and trends.
7. **Designed against deception.** AI reading outside content (student searches, emails, web pages) can be
   tricked by text crafted for it. Tools never combine broad data access, untrusted content, and the power to
   act outward in one place, and a person approves every action.

### Where trust is thinner (tell leadership this too)

- "No training" is a contract, not physics. We verify the plan at adoption and renewal.
- "De-identified" is not "anonymous". A child can be recognized from a few combined details.
- The human is our strongest and weakest link. A well-meaning staff member pasting a class list into a free
  chatbot is the biggest realistic risk. Training matters as much as settings.

## 7. Meetings and recordings

AI meeting recorders may be used for [PERMITTED MEETING TYPES: staff meetings, vendor calls, planning].
Never for meetings about a specific student, family, employee, or counseling matter, and never without
telling participants.

## 8. Accountability

- **Designated reviewer:** [NAME / ROLE]. Reviews new tools, connectors, plugins and anything built that
  touches Confidential or Restricted data. Tools built by the reviewer get a second review by [ROLE].
- **Sanctioned-tool list:** kept in `ai-rules.md` rule 29 by the reviewer. Additions are reported to
  [LEADERSHIP BODY].
- **Incidents:** suspected misuse or exposure goes to the reviewer the same day.
- **Departures and role changes:** revoke the AI seat, rotate any shared credential the person held, delete
  local working files.
- **Review cadence:** this policy is reviewed [ANNUALLY] and whenever a sanctioned tool changes materially.

## 9. Decisions for leadership

1. Adopt the four tiers and the approve-before-acting rule.
2. Approve the student-monitoring use in section 5, including who may run it and retention.
3. Confirm the legal review of student-privacy obligations.
4. Name the designated reviewer and who may amend the sanctioned-tool list.
5. Decide the meeting-recording rule.
6. Decide what families are told about AI use with school data.
