# Church AI use policy: a template

A complete AI use policy a church's elders or board can adapt and adopt, plus a short notice for members. It was
generalized from a policy one church's elders were reviewing in November 2026, after a round of outside review.
It works with any AI service; nothing in it requires Claude or this kit.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [`ai-use-policy.md`](ai-use-policy.md) | The policy, for leaders to read, amend and vote on |
| [`members-notice.md`](members-notice.md) | A one-page notice for your website, in plain words |

## What it covers

- **Convictions from Scripture,** so every rule carries its reason.
- **Access follows access.** AI sees only what the person using it could already see, with three deliberate
  exceptions: counseling, giving and children.
- **Eight bright lines,** including no church information about people in personal AI accounts, and AI never as
  the first responder to someone in crisis.
- **A person approves** every message, post and record change before it happens. No automatic replies.
- **Preaching and teaching:** AI as a commentary on the desk, never the author, for everyone who teaches with the
  church's endorsement. Three lanes for the words a church sends.
- **Counseling in three lanes:** operations open, help with case notes only under four conditions, AI as a
  counselor never.
- **Meetings and recordings:** consent where no is easy, deletion on request, transcripts kept for a short time,
  the reviewed summary as the record.
- **Helpers in three levels:** try freely, check first, full review by a person.
- **A phased rollout,** low-risk uses first, sensitive ones only once the safeguards are shown to work.
- **Review, suspend, revoke,** questions for your lawyer and insurer, and the members' notice.

## How to adapt it

1. **Read it once, whole.** It is long because every rule carries its reason; the reasons are what let staff
   apply it to cases it never names.
2. **Fill in the brackets.** `[CHURCH NAME]`, your systems (church database, accounting, payroll), who your
   leaders are, who reviews tools, and the numbers: how long recordings are kept, how long Phase 1 runs, the
   smallest group a total may describe.
3. **Make the choices.** Each `> Choice:` note marks a decision that belongs to your leaders, not to whoever
   is drafting. The big ones: your convictions and Scripture, whether your pastors see individual giving,
   whether you have a counseling ministry, and which uses start in which phase.
4. **Ask counsel.** Section 17 lists the legal questions, including your state's recording consent rules. Send
   them before the vote, not after.
5. **Adopt, then publish the notice.** Change any line in `members-notice.md` that your leaders changed in the
   policy, so it never promises more than the policy does.
6. **Teach it, then enforce what must be true.** Written rules teach; settings and access enforce. If you use
   Claude, the rest of this kit turns the policy into rules Claude loads in every conversation (see the main
   [README](../README.md)).
